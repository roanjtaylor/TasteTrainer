description: Teach me — about this item, this dataset, this world, or whatever I name
---
Teach me about what I'm looking at (or about what I name below). I am training my eye, so the point is what to LOOK AT and why it matters, not a biography.

- **An item:** what makes it defining — what it did first, what it changed, what it does better than its peers. Where it sits in the field's lineage: what came before it, what learned from it. Then compare it to two or three of its neighbours in this dataset and tell me what to notice when I see them side by side. What would an expert see in it that I would miss?
- **A dataset:** how this field really divides and how it moved over time — the turning points, the schools, the rivalries. Use the items that are here as the examples, in the order I should look at them, and say which few to study first. Tell me what the dataset's spread of years and makers already shows, and what it can't show yet.
- **A world:** how it divides into fields, what the axes of its map mean in practice, and where I should spend time next given what I have built.
- **Something I name:** the same, grounded in whatever of it is in my datasets.

Draw on what you know; use the web where a fact matters and you are not certain of it. Be concrete and specific — a name, a year, a detail to look for — never generic praise. Write it as a short essay I can read in a few minutes, with the most important thing first.

This is a reading, not a change: don't stage anything. If you notice something wrong in the data while you're at it (a year, an attribution, an item that doesn't belong), tell me at the end in a line each and I'll decide whether to ask you to fix it.
